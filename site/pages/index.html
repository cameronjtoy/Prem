Open-source electronic lab notebook

A lab notebook that stays on your own machines.

Experiments, protocols, runs and samples, kept as plain files your lab owns. Every save is kept, records can be signed and witnessed, and the whole lab can share one notebook from a server you run.

Download Prem View the source

Free for macOS, Windows and Linux.

Your data stays with the lab

Notebooks are folders of markdown and attachments, on your computer or a server you run. No account with us, no cloud, and nothing locked into a format only Prem can read.

Built for the bench

Protocols with checkable steps. Runs that record when each step was done and what deviated. Samples with IDs. Gels, plate reads and traces attached to the entry they belong to.

Records you can stand behind

Every save is kept in a tamper-evident history. Sign an entry to lock it, have a colleague witness it, and change it later only through an amendment with a reason.

One person or a whole lab

Start with a folder on your laptop. Add the lab server when you want shared protocols and per-person notebooks: students write their own, the PI reads everything.

Protocols become runs

Start a run from any protocol. Prem copies its materials and steps into your notebook, records the time as you tick each step, and keeps a log of deviations. The protocol lists every time it was used.

Sign, witness, amend

A signed entry is locked, on your machine and on the lab server. The history is a hash chain, so altering a past version or signature is detected, and Prem flags signed entries edited outside the app.

Signatures are recorded attestations, not cryptographic signatures yet. Prem is not certified for 21 CFR Part 11.

Find anything

Search every entry, protocol and sample at once, and jump straight to the line that matched. Link entries with [[double brackets]] and see what links back.

Where Prem is going

Now: pilot

Installers for every platform, PDF export with signatures, a guided lab-server setup and notebook (.ipynb) previews.

Next: lab workflows

Samples and projects moving through stages, with tasks, assignees and a board. Jupyter analyses that write figures and results back into the entry.

Later: analysis in the notebook

Python cells inside entries, run by a kernel Prem manages, with outputs saved as part of the signed record.
